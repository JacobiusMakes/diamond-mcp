# FTC Definition of Diamond (2018)

*Lab Grown Diamonds*

**In 2018 the U.S. Federal Trade Commission revised its Jewelry Guides and removed the word "natural" from the definition of a diamond, formally recognizing that a diamond grown in a laboratory is still a diamond.**

The Federal Trade Commission's Jewelry Guides are the rules that govern how diamonds and jewelry can be described and marketed in the United States. For decades the guides defined a diamond, in effect, as a natural mineral made of carbon crystallized in the isometric system. The word "natural" carried an important implication: that only a stone taken from the earth could properly be called a diamond.

In 2018 the FTC revised the guides and removed "natural" from the definition (FTC Jewelry Guides, 2018). The reasoning was straightforward. A Lab Grown Diamond is the same material as a mined one, identical in chemistry, crystal structure, and physical and optical properties, so defining a diamond as something only the earth can make was no longer accurate. Under the revised guides, a Lab Grown Diamond is a diamond.

The FTC paired that with guidance on honesty. Sellers should clearly disclose when a stone is a Lab Grown Diamond, not mined, and terms such as "laboratory-grown," "laboratory-created," and "[manufacturer name]-created" are acceptable ways to say so (FTC Jewelry Guides, 2018). The Commission also cautioned against language designed to imply that a Lab Grown Diamond is not a real diamond (FTC Jewelry Guides, 2018).

This is why "a Lab Grown Diamond is a real diamond" is a statement of regulatory fact, not marketing. The material was always the same; in 2018 the definition was updated to match. Origin remains a genuine and disclosable difference, but it's a difference in where the stone came from, not in what it is.

## Sources

- The FTC revised its Jewelry Guides and removed the word 'natural' from the definition of a diamond. (Source: Federal Trade Commission, Guides for the Jewelry, Precious Metals, and Pewter Industries, final rule, 83 FR 40665 (Federal Register), 2018-08-16. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2018/08/16/2018-17454/guides-for-the-jewelry-precious-metals-and-pewter-industries)
- The FTC advised sellers to disclose lab-grown origin and accepted qualifiers such as 'laboratory-grown', 'laboratory-created', and '[manufacturer name]-created'. (Source: 16 CFR 23.25(b), FTC Jewelry Guides (as revised 2018), cross-referenced from the Note to 16 CFR 23.12(c), via Cornell Legal Information Institute, 2018. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/23.25)
- In its Statement of Basis and Purpose for the 2018 Jewelry Guides revision, the FTC stated that if a marketer uses 'synthetic' to imply that a competitor's lab-grown diamond is not an actual diamond, this would be deceptive (printed p. 92, PDF p. 98). (Source: Federal Trade Commission, Statement of Basis and Purpose: Final Revisions to the Jewelry Guides (PDF posted August 8, 2018, printed p. 92; linked from the FTC landing page dated July 24, 2018 at ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statement-basis-purpose-final-revisions-jewelry-guides), 2018-07-24. https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/public_statements/1393857/g71001_jewelry_guides_statement_of_basis_and_purpose_final_8-8-18.pdf)
- On July 24, 2018 the FTC approved final revisions to the Jewelry Guides, including changes to the definition of 'diamond' and to qualifying claims about man-made gemstone products. (Source: US Federal Trade Commission press release, "FTC Approves Final Revisions to Jewelry Guides", 2018-07-24. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2018/07/ftc-approves-final-revisions-jewelry-guides)
