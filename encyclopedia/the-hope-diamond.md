# The Hope Diamond

*History and myths*

**The Hope Diamond is a 45.52-carat fancy deep grayish-blue diamond, colored by trace boron, on public display at the Smithsonian's National Museum of Natural History in Washington.**

The Hope Diamond is a 45.52 carat fancy deep grayish-blue diamond, one of the largest blue diamonds known. It has been on public display at the Smithsonian's National Museum of Natural History in Washington since the jeweler Harry Winston donated it in 1958. Winston famously sent it to the museum by registered mail.

Its blue color comes from tiny amounts of boron in the crystal. One striking property is that after exposure to ultraviolet light the stone briefly glows, or phosphoresces, a fiery red. The Hope also has a long, documented history. Its color and size trace back to a large blue diamond that the French merchant Jean-Baptiste Tavernier brought from India and sold to King Louis XIV in 1668, a stone recut into the French crown gem known as the French Blue. That gem disappeared during the French Revolution. In 2005 a Smithsonian-led team announced computer-modeling research concluding that the Hope Diamond was cut from the missing French Blue. In 2009 a study of a lead cast of the French Blue, found in the mineral collection of the Paris natural history museum, confirmed that the Hope could have been recut from it.

You will often hear that the diamond is cursed. That part is a legend, a story popularized by publicity in the early twentieth century rather than a documented fact, and the museum itself treats it as folklore. What makes the stone remarkable is real enough without it: an unusually large blue diamond, a rare boron-blue color, and a paper trail running back more than three centuries.

## Sources

- The blue color is attributed to trace boron; the stone is a type IIb diamond that phosphoresces a strong red after short-wave UV; Tavernier sold it to Louis XIV in 1668 and it became the French Blue. (Source: Smithsonian Institution, History of the Hope Diamond (Spotlight), accessed 2026-09-08. https://www.si.edu/spotlight/hope-diamond/history)
- The Hope Diamond weighs 45.52 carats (measured out of its setting in 1975), was graded fancy deep grayish-blue by GIA in 1996, phosphoresces red under UV, and has been at the Smithsonian since Harry Winston donated it in 1958, arriving by registered mail in a plain brown package. (Source: Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History, Hope Diamond, accessed 2026-09-08. https://naturalhistory.si.edu/explore/collections/hope-diamond)
- In February 2005 a research team led by Smithsonian gem curator Jeffrey Post and gem cutter Steven Attaway, with Scott Sucher and Nancy Attaway, reported a year-long analysis using historical sketches, 1700-era French measurements and computer models, concluding that the Hope Diamond was cut from the French Blue of the French crown jewels; the report describes the 45.52 carat stone as the world's largest blue diamond. (Source: Associated Press via NBC News, "Hope Diamond originally came from French crown", 2005-02-10. https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna6946421)
- Computer models of a rediscovered lead cast of the French Blue and of the Hope Diamond confirm that the Hope could have been recut from the French Blue; a catalog entry found with the cast suggests Henry Philip Hope may have owned the French Blue after its 1792 theft. (Source: François Farges, Scott Sucher, Herbert Horovitz and Jean-Marc Fourcault, "The French Blue and the Hope: New Data from the Discovery of a Historical Lead Cast," Gems & Gemology, Spring 2009, Vol. 45, No. 1 (doi 10.5741/GEMS.45.1.4), 2009. https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/spring-2009-french-blue-hope-diamond-farges)
