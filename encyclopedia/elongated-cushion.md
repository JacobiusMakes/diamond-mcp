# elongated cushion

*Cuts and shapes*

**An elongated cushion is a cushion cut with a rectangular rather than square outline, stretched so its length clearly exceeds its width while keeping the soft rounded corners and pillow silhouette. It is a longer, more finger-flattering version of the standard cushion.**

An elongated cushion is not a separate faceting family; it is a cushion cut, a modified brilliant with rounded corners and curved sides, drawn out to a longer length-to-width ratio. Where a classic cushion is close to square, an elongated cushion is drawn out so its length clearly exceeds its width, giving it a rectangular pillow shape; the length and width on the grading report give the exact ratio for any individual stone.

Face-up, that extra length does two useful things. Against a square cushion of the same weight and depth, the longer outline can spread a little more face-up area, and it lengthens and slims the look of the finger, the same reason people choose ovals and elongated radiants. Do not count on the first effect: cushions as a family are often cut deep, and GIA notes that their deeper proportions can make them appear smaller than other shapes of the same weight. The faceting can be the chunky style or the crushed-ice style, exactly as with a square cushion, so the sparkle character depends on the individual stone.

On durability the elongated cushion usually keeps the standard cushion's advantage: its corners are normally rounded rather than sharp, so there is rarely a fragile tip to chip, and it sits securely in a setting. Two caveats from GIA: some modern cushions are cut with pointed corners, and a cushion's girdle is often thicker at the corners than along the sides, so a stone with an extremely thin girdle can chip along those sides; a bezel protects such a stone better than prongs.

The practical point is that length is a preference, not a grade, and there is no single correct ratio. Some people want just a hint of stretch, others a long, narrow rectangle, and two stones of the same carat weight can look quite different depending on how far each is elongated. It is worth looking at the actual length-to-width ratio, and ideally the stone in person, since the number alone does not tell you how it will feel on the hand.
