# asscher cut

*Cuts and shapes*

**An Asscher cut is a square step cut with cut corners, essentially a square emerald cut with deeper, more pronounced steps that draw the eye toward the center in an X or windmill pattern. It reads as a nearly octagonal square with concentric squares of light.**

The Asscher belongs to the step-cut family, the same family as the emerald cut. Its outline is square rather than rectangular, with the four corners cut off so that it appears almost octagonal. The step facets are usually cut steeper and deeper than an emerald cut's, and the pavilion draws down to a small center, producing concentric squares and an X-shaped play of light when you look straight down through the table.

Face-up, an Asscher gives the same restrained, glassy character as an emerald cut rather than bright sparkle. The layered squares create a sense of depth, sometimes called a hall-of-mirrors effect. Being square, it covers the finger differently from an elongated emerald cut, reading as compact and geometric.

As with any step cut, the open, mirror-like facets show the inside of the stone clearly, so inclusions and body color are easier to see than in a brilliant cut. The practical note is the same as for the emerald cut: favor a higher clarity and color grade than you would for a sparkly round, because there is nowhere for a flaw to hide.

On durability the cut corners work in your favor, removing the sharp ninety-degree points that would otherwise be fragile, so an Asscher sets securely. A well-cut Asscher is prized for symmetry: the concentric squares should be perfectly centered, and any tilt is easy to spot when you view the stone face-up.

## Sources

- The Asscher cut was developed in 1902 by Joseph Asscher; it is nearly octagonal in outline and carries 58 large, wide-set step facets in three rows on the crown and three on the pavilion. (Source: GIA 4Cs blog, The Asscher Cut: A Cut of Royalty and Reinvention, accessed 2026-09-08. https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/the-asscher-cut-a-cut-of-royalty-and-reinvention/)
- An Asscher is a square step cut with a hall-of-mirrors look; because its facets are large and open, clarity and color show more, so higher grades (VS2 and G or better) are recommended, the same advice GIA gives for the emerald cut. (Source: GIA 4Cs blog, Guide to Diamond Engagement Ring Shapes, accessed 2026-09-08. https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/guide-diamond-shapes-engagement-rings/)
- Gemological laboratories identify the Asscher as a Square Emerald Cut or Cut-Cornered Square Step Cut; the 1902 Asscher Brothers patent has expired. (Source: IGI, Ultimate Guide to Different Types of Diamond Cuts, accessed 2026-09-08. https://www.igi.org/understanding-diamond-cut-shapes-a-guide-to-different-types-of-diamond-cuts/)
- Square step cuts need higher clarity because inclusions are easier to see than in brilliant cuts. (Source: GIA 4Cs blog, Expert Tips for Buying Fancy Shaped Diamonds (Holiday Buying Guide), accessed 2026-09-08. https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/purchasing-fancy-shaped-diamonds-holiday-buying-guide/)
