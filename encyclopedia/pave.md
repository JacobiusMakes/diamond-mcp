# pave

*Settings and metals*

**Pave (pronounced pah-vay, from the French for paved) is a technique that packs many tiny diamonds close together, held by minute metal beads, so the surface reads as continuous sparkle rather than individual stones. Micro-pave uses especially small stones.**

In pave, a setter drills seats for a row or field of small diamonds and raises tiny beads of metal from the surrounding surface to hold each stone at its corners. Because the stones sit close and the beads are minute, the eye sees a paved sheet of light rather than separate settings, which is where the name comes from. Pave most often runs along the shoulders of a band, but it can also cover the sides of a setting, the halo around a center stone, or the whole surface of a design.

Pave adds a great deal of sparkle for relatively little carat weight, and it makes a center stone look larger by ringing it with light. The trade-off is care. The stones are held by small beads, so an occasional pave diamond can loosen or fall out over years of hard wear. This is a normal maintenance item, not a defect, and a jeweler can reset a lost stone easily. Having the beads looked at when you get your prongs checked keeps a pave ring in good shape. Because the band surface is filled with stones, a fully paved band is harder to resize without disturbing them, so it is often made in a fixed size or sized only in a plain section. Related styles include micro-pave, which uses even smaller stones for a smoother field of light, and channel setting, where small stones sit between two walls of metal instead of under beads.

## Sources

- Pave is a bead setting: each small stone sits in a recessed hole and is held by small metal beads at its girdle; stones are placed close together so the piece looks 'paved' with diamonds; pave stones can fall out with hard wear. (Source: GIA 4Cs blog, Side Stone Settings for Engagement Rings, 2012-12-13. https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/side-stone-settings-for-engagement-rings/)
- Channel setting holds small stones edge to edge between two walls of metal, unlike pave beads. (Source: GIA 4Cs blog, Side Stone Settings for Engagement Rings, 2012-12-13. https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/side-stone-settings-for-engagement-rings/)
- Pave rings need occasional maintenance to keep stones secure, and pave bands are harder to resize because adjusting the ring can disturb the small stones. (Source: GIA 4Cs blog, GIA Guide to Engagement Ring Settings, 2026-08-14. https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/guide-to-ring-settings/)
- Pave and channel-set rings cannot be resized easily. (Source: GIA 4Cs blog, How to Determine Ring Size: Tips and Ring Size Chart, 2018-01-18. https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/how-to-determine-ring-size-tips-and-ring-size-chart/)
